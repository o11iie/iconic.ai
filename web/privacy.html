This policy describes what the Slate Android app and its backend collect, why, how long it is kept, and who it is shared with. It is written to match what the software actually does. If the app changes, this page changes with it.

1. The short version

  • Slate requires an account: an email address, a password, a handle and a display name.
  • Slate stores what you follow, watchlist, post and ask, because those are the product.
  • Slate does not collect your location, contacts, photos, microphone, camera, calendar, SMS, call logs, installed-app list or device advertising identifier.
  • Slate does not sell personal information, and does not share it with data brokers or advertisers.
  • This version of Slate contains no advertising SDK and no third-party analytics SDK.
  • You can delete your account and data from inside the app, or from this website, without contacting anyone.

2.3 Ask Slate (AI)

When you send a message to Ask Slate, Slate stores the conversation — your messages and the assistant's replies — so you can see your history and so the assistant has context. Slate also stores a per-day counter of how many messages you have sent, to enforce the free and Pro daily limits.

2.4 Subscription information

If you buy Slate Pro, Slate stores the Google Play purchase token, the product you bought, the subscription status Google reports, and the expiry and renewal state. Slate never receives or stores your payment card, billing address or any other payment instrument — that transaction happens entirely inside Google Play.

2.5 Product analytics

Slate records a small set of first-party product events — for example that a search happened, a title was viewed, a paywall was shown, a purchase completed — with the event name, a timestamp, and non-identifying properties such as a media type. These are sent to Slate's own backend only. They are not sent to any third-party analytics service, and Slate does not use an advertising identifier. If you delete your account, these events are kept but permanently detached from you, so they survive only as anonymous counts.

2.6 Technical data

Like any internet service, Slate's servers process your IP address and the request itself in order to answer it, to apply rate limits, and to detect abuse. Slate does not build advertising or cross-site profiles from this.

3. What Slate does not collect

Slate requests only two Android permissions: INTERNET and com.android.vending.BILLING. It explicitly blocks location, camera and microphone permissions from being added by any dependency. Slate has no access to your location, contacts, photos, files, camera, microphone, calendar, SMS, call logs, health data, or the list of apps installed on your device, and does not read your device advertising ID.

5. What other people can see

Your handle, display name, optional profile image, and anything you post or comment are visible to other signed-in Slate users. Your email address, your watchlist, your follows, your Ask Slate conversations, your notification history and your subscription status are not.

6. How long Slate keeps data

  • While your account exists: for as long as you keep it.
  • Sessions: refresh tokens are stored hashed and expire; signing out revokes them.
  • After you delete your account: your account row, email, password hash, sessions, follows, watchlist, journeys, notifications, Ask Slate conversations, reactions and entitlement record are deleted immediately and irreversibly. Your posts and comments have their text removed and their authorship reassigned to an anonymous [deleted] account, so replies written by other people are not destroyed. Reports you filed stay with moderators but no longer point at you. Analytics events are detached from you and remain only as anonymous counts.
  • Backups: deleted data may persist in encrypted backups for a short period before those backups rotate out, and is not restored to the live service.

7. Your choices and rights

  • Delete your account: in the app under Profile → Settings → Delete account, or from this website. No email or support ticket is required.
  • Turn off notifications: all of them, or individually by type, in Settings.
  • Correct your details: change your display name, handle, genres and spoiler sensitivity in the app.
  • Access or export: email and we will provide a copy of your data.
  • Depending on where you live, you may also have rights to object to or restrict processing, or to complain to your local data protection authority. Email to exercise them.

Deleting your Slate account does not cancel a Slate Pro subscription. Google Play controls the billing relationship — cancel it in the Play Store under Payments & subscriptions, or you will continue to be charged.

8. Children

Slate is not directed to children. You must be at least 13 years old — or the minimum age of digital consent where you live, if that is higher — to create a Slate account. Slate does not knowingly collect personal information from children below that age. If you believe a child has created an account, email and we will delete it.

9. Security

Passwords are hashed with bcrypt and never stored in readable form. Access tokens are short-lived and refresh tokens are stored hashed, rotated on use, and revocable. All traffic between the app and Slate's servers is encrypted in transit; release builds refuse to communicate over plaintext HTTP. API credentials for TMDB, IGDB, OpenAI and Google Play are held only on Slate's servers and are never present in the app on your device. No system is perfectly secure, but these are the measures in place.

10. International transfers

Slate's servers and the third parties listed in section 4 may process data in countries other than your own, including the United States. Where required, transfers rely on appropriate safeguards such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

11. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that materially affects you, the effective date at the top changes and Slate notifies you in the app before the change takes effect.
